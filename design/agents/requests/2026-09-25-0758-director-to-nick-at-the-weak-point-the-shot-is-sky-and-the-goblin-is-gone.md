## What

This is what a player sees the moment the Frog reaches the weak point — the
payoff of the whole climb — at play size, this morning:

![[frames/director/2026-09-25-0753-director-sigil.png]]

- The top four-fifths of the frame is empty sky.
- The beast is a black-and-orange lump in the bottom-left corner, cut off by

- The "Attack 7" sign floats alone in the sky on the left, with nothing under

- The Goblin is nowhere. It is fifteen units below on the ground.

For comparison, the resting shot at the same moment this morning — the one
your drawing is about, and the one the fixer and I have been working — has
the beast whole, both hunters in view and the path between them:

![[frames/director/2026-09-25-0753-director-resting.png]]

**Why this is your call and not theirs.** Two of the agents have decided the
Goblin being off-screen at the weak point is intended: the playtester's
screenshot check prints "out of frame by design" for it (03:22 this morning),
and the artist ticked "both hunters are always findable, including
mid-climb" on the bar yesterday, arguing the party panel at top-left is what
keeps the partner findable. The frame the artist ticked it on was taken
before you opened the gap; back then the Goblin stood beside the Frog. I
have not found anywhere you said the partner may leave the frame at the
weak point, and your camera words ("the other hunter off to one side, never
dead centre") assume it is in view. So I am asking rather than letting it
stand.

**Options:**

- **Reframe on the beast (my pick):** at the weak point the camera sits
  close behind the Frog looking along the beast's back and down toward its
  head, so the beast fills the frame, the weak point is in the middle, and
  the Goblin is small on the ground below. Trade-off: it is a second camera
  pose for the fixer to tune after the resting one, and the checks that were
  just rebuilt around the current pose get re-baselined.
- **Leave it as it is:** the party panel tracks the Goblin; the shot stays a
  pulled-back view from above. Trade-off: the payoff shot stays mostly sky,
  and the partner disappears from the world exactly when the two of you are
  meant to be coordinating.

Either way I recommend it goes AFTER the resting camera row you already have
(the gap-versus-lens question), not before; this is only so the answer is on
record before anyone builds toward the wrong one.
